Why Now
Four Converging Forces
01 · Platform Consolidation
The dominant players are merging — and trust is the casualty
$2.2B
Home Depot acquired HomeAdvisor in 2024. Thumbtack acquired Angie's List home services division in May 2025. Platform consolidation optimizes for volume, not trust — creating a structural gap for a credentialing-first entrant.
Straits Research / Technavio, 2025 · Thumbtack FTC filing, May 2025
02 · Consumer Trust Collapse
Existing platforms carry a 14% trust rating — lower than word of mouth
41%
41% of homeowners report being deceived by a service provider. Only 14% trust third-party platforms vs. 61% who trust word-of-mouth. Nearly 30% have had a negative experience via a platform. The deficit is measurable and growing.
Leaf Home Survey, Feb 2025 · Roto-Rooter Consumer Survey, April 2024
03 · Regulatory Escalation
Enforcement is rising — compliance creates a built-in sales channel
73
Delaware recorded 73 home improvement fraud arrests in 2024 — a 5-year high. Tennessee's contractor board levied $990K in civil penalties from 884 complaints. Stricter enforcement converts trust verification from a value-add into a professional necessity.
Spotlight Delaware, April 2025 · TN Board for Licensing Contractors, 2024
04 · AI-Enabled Verification at Scale
Continuous monitoring is now cheap enough to productize
726K
726,026 US remodeling businesses in 2025 (+4.2% YoY). AI-powered license verification, continuous background monitoring, and review authenticity scoring remove the unit economics barrier that made earlier trust attempts unscalable.
Zippia Industry Statistics, 2025 · ShieldHub / Checkr pricing benchmarks, 2024–2025
