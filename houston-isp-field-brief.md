## PART I — MISSION CONTEXT

### What We're Building

uMODA is a Quote Transparency API built for the independent auto repair market. It creates structured, time-stamped documentation of the full quote-to-bill lifecycle: initial estimate, scope changes, authorization events, parts sourcing decisions, and final billing. The platform gives honest shops a way to prove their honesty — to every customer, before and after every repair.

### Why We're Building It

The independent auto repair market has a trust deficit — not because most shops are dishonest, but because consumers have no infrastructure to distinguish the honest from the dishonest. Every verbal estimate that escalates looks identical to consumer fraud. Every teardown revision looks like a bait-and-switch. Every communication gap looks like avoidance. Honest shops pay the full reputational cost of an industry-wide opacity problem they did not create.

uMODA is not a consumer complaint tool. It is shop-side transparency infrastructure that turns documented process into competitive advantage — and turns a 1-star review from a misunderstood verbal estimate into a defensible, timestamped record the shop can point to.

## PART II — THE SP PAIN FRAMEWORK

### The 17 Quote Change Drivers (Shop-Side Perspective)

The uMODA platform is built to address 17 mechanisms by which repair quotes diverge from final bills. Fifteen are attributable to shop behavior or structural market conditions — including several that trap **honest shops** in disputes they did not create. Two drivers are legitimately external: supply chain inflation and consumer-supplied parts.

For field conversations, the following six drivers are most directly relevant to the shops on this list — shops the data identifies as operating with integrity, but without the documentation infrastructure that would protect them when disputes arise.

---

#### Driver 10 — Communication Breakdown
*The everyday structural risk every shop carries*

62% of incoming calls to the average auto repair shop go unanswered (industry benchmark). When a shop can't reach a customer mid-repair and proceeds on assumption — or when a customer can't reach the shop for a status update — the conditions for a quote dispute are set without any bad actor involved. The shop did the work. The customer disputes the price. Neither party has a timestamped record.

**Quantified cost:** Phone-tag approval delays add 4–8 hours to average job completion. Missed revenue from declined approvals the shop never communicated: measurable per RO on any shop management system.

---

#### Driver 8 — Authorization Disputes
*The documentation gap that creates legal exposure*

Texas law (Tex. Occ. Code § 2302.052) requires written authorization for any work exceeding $100 above a written estimate. Verbal authorization is legally insufficient. Most shops operating without a digital workflow are one customer dispute away from an unenforceable authorization position — even when they completed exactly the work the customer asked for.

**Quantified cost:** A single mechanic's lien dispute in Texas can cost $500–$5,000 in legal fees and storage while the vehicle sits. That does not count the review damage or the customer loss.

---

#### Driver 13 — Verbal Estimate Substitution
*The honest shop's reputational exposure*

A shop quotes $800 verbally, completes the job, bills $950 with documented justification for the change. The customer posts a 1-star review citing "bait and switch." The shop has no timestamped record of the verbal quote, the scope change conversation, or the customer's informal approval. The Texas AG names verbal estimate disputes as a recognized deceptive practice — but the shop has no way to distinguish itself from that pattern without documentation.

**Quantified cost:** A 1-star review citing a price dispute costs approximately 14–30 new customers per month in reduced Google Business Profile conversion (Harvard Business School research; current algorithm-weighted impact likely higher).

---

#### Driver 9 — Teardown / Hidden-Damage Scenarios
*The legitimate discovery that looks like extortion*

When a technician opens a transmission and finds a failed bearing that wasn't visible externally, that's a real finding requiring a real scope revision. Without timestamped photo documentation of what teardown found and when the customer was notified, that legitimate revision is indistinguishable — to the customer, to a regulator, to a Yelp reviewer — from the exploitative version. Both the honest and dishonest shop produce the same customer experience in the absence of documentation.

**Quantified cost:** Undocumented teardown revisions of $300–$500 that produce disputes cost the shop the full RO value plus future business from that customer's network.

---

#### Driver 11 — Hidden Fees / Pricing Opacity
*The disclosure gap that damages trust at pickup*

Shop supply fees, environmental disposal charges, and inspection surcharges added at billing — not at estimate — produce a trust rupture even when every charge is legitimate. The FTC requires advance notice of miscellaneous charges. Most shops add them post-estimate by habit or software default. The customer who expected $435 and paid $472 will write a review about the $37 gap.

**Quantified cost:** Small fee surprises drive review events disproportionate to their dollar value. A $37 shop supply fee disclosed at pickup generates the same emotional response as a $200 unauthorized charge.

---

#### Driver 1 — Quote-to-Final-Bill Price Escalation
*The gap any consumer can verify without technical knowledge*

Texas law anchors written authorization at $100 above the written estimate. AAA consumer guidance suggests 10% as a perceived threshold for dispute. Any shop billing consistently above the estimate without a timestamped change-order trail is exposed — even when the escalation is fully justified by the work performed.

**Quantified cost:** Every disputed RO that results in a write-down costs the shop 35–50% of average RO value. Every disputed RO that produces a negative review costs the equivalent of 5–15 future ROs in lost referral traffic.

## PART III — uMODA VALUE PROPOSITION FOR ISPs

### The Platform Promise

uMODA creates the documentation layer that currently does not exist between shop and customer:

- **Time-stamped estimates** — every quote anchored with a timestamp and a customer-facing record
- **Change order tracking** — scope revisions documented with cause, customer notification, and authorization capture
- **Digital authorization** — written consent captured within the platform before work proceeds
- **Communication log** — every customer contact attempt, update, and response recorded
- **Teardown documentation** — photo evidence of pre-repair condition and in-progress findings
- **Transparent billing** — fees disclosed at estimate, not at pickup

### The Pitch in One Sentence

> *"You're already doing this right. uMODA makes sure everyone can see that."*

### For Shops Already Using a DVI Platform (TekMetric, Shop-Ware, Mitchell1)

The conversation is different here. These shops are already capturing digital inspections. The outreach angle: uMODA extends transparency into the quote-to-authorization layer, creating a defensible record that DVI alone does not provide — and surfaces it as a consumer-facing trust signal.

## PART IV — OUTREACH FRAMEWORK (PRE-VISIT)

### Contact Strategy by Tier

**Tier A — Digital-Native (Website + Active Platform Signals)**
*Channel priority: Email → Phone → Visit*

Send a personalized email 5–7 days before the field visit. Reference something specific to the shop — their specialty, their online reviews, a particular operational practice. Frame the email as "we're coming to your area and want 15 minutes." Do not lead with the product.

> **Subject template:** *[Specialty] shops in [area] — brief conversation before we arrive?*
>
> **Body frame:** "We're building something that speaks directly to quote disputes in the Houston ISP market, and we've done our research. I noticed [specific detail about shop]. Would you have 15 minutes when we're in the area [date]? We're not selling software — we're validating a problem we know exists, and we'd value your perspective."

**Tier B — Digitally Visible, Named Owner**
*Channel priority: Phone → Walk-in with notice*

Call 3–5 days before. Ask for the named contact by first name. Keep it under 2 minutes. Goal: set the expectation that someone is coming — not to sell.

> **Phone script:** "Hi, is [name] available? ... Hi [name], I'm with the uMODA team. We're doing field visits in [area] next [week] and meeting with shops we've identified as doing things the right way. We'd love 15 minutes — no product pitch, we're early-stage and we're learning. Would [day/time] work?"

**Tier C — No Digital Presence, Walk-In**
*Channel priority: Direct walk-in with leave-behind*

Bring a one-page overview of the uMODA pain framework — the 6 primary drivers, the Texas statutory anchors, the cost data. Open with: *"We're documenting how honest shops in Houston deal with quote disputes."* Let the owner teach you their version of the problem. Listen first. Leave the one-pager.
