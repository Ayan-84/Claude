# Celebrity & Cultural Figure Investment Vehicles
### Focused on Black and Traditionally Marginalized Communities

**Date:** May 2026
**Status:** Research Document — Landscape Survey
**Scope:** Investment vehicles controlled by or structurally aligned with Black and other traditionally marginalized cultural figures; relevant to identifying outreach targets whose thesis resonates with situated fairness, community ownership, and the cultural value gap

---

## What This Survey Covers

These are not standard VC firms. They are investment vehicles, funds, platforms, and ownership structures operated by or in partnership with celebrities, athletes, and cultural figures — predominantly from Black communities. They are evaluated here because:

1. Several are active investors in the exact infrastructure category uMODA occupies
2. Many share the same "cultural value created without ownership" thesis that underlies uMODA's situated fairness principle
3. Their LP networks, warm intro pathways, and community credibility are structurally distinct from traditional VC pipelines

---

## THE NAMED THREE

These three funds are the most institutionally formal celebrity investment vehicles with direct thesis alignment to the structural problems uMODA addresses.

---

### QueensBridge Venture Partners — Nas (Nasir Jones)
**Website:** queensbridgeventures.com
**Co-founders:** Nas and Anthony Saleh (longtime manager)

Named after the Queensbridge Houses in Long Island City, NY — the largest public housing project in the United States, where Nas grew up. The name is the thesis.

**Fund characteristics:** Seed through Series A. $250K–$2M checks. Still active; Saleh manages operational diligence.

**Notable portfolio:** Lyft, Genius (fmr. Rap Genius), Coinbase (seed — became one of the most valuable celebrity VC positions in history), Ring (acquired by Amazon $1B+), Robinhood, JUMP Bikes, Dropbox, Casper, Splice, Warby Parker. Estimated portfolio returns in excess of $400M.

**Why QueensBridge is structurally distinct:**
- Sourcing thesis is genuinely community-based: look for companies where urban populations were or would become the primary users, and where cultural credibility opens doors traditional VC cannot
- Ring was the example: home security for neighborhoods where security mattered most and was least accessible
- This is the same thesis logic as uMODA's situated fairness principle — the communities most affected by the Problematic Triad are the same communities Nas was investing for

**Relevance to uMODA:** QueensBridge understands that underserved communities are not the charity case — they are the underserved market. This frames auto care consumer protection as a market opportunity, not a social good.

---

### Marcy Venture Partners (MVP) — Jay-Z (Shawn Carter)
**Website:** marcyventureink.com
**Co-founders:** Jay Brown (CEO, Roc Nation), Larry Marcus (fmr. Walden VC)

Named after Marcy Houses in Bedford-Stuyvesant, Brooklyn. Jay Brown and Larry Marcus run operations; Jay-Z is the cultural architect.

**Fund characteristics:** Series A and beyond. ~$85M Fund I; ~$85M Fund II (2023). Culture-first consumer thesis — backs brands with authentic cultural roots that can scale into mainstream adoption.

**Notable portfolio:** Oatly (Nasdaq IPO 2021), BookClub, Super Coffee, Camp, Flowspace, Slate Milk, D'USSE Cognac (acquired by Bacardi), Armand de Brignac Champagne (acquired by LVMH $600M+), TIDAL (sold to Square/Block $297M in stock).

**Structural note on Oatly:** Oatly's core market is communities with higher rates of lactose intolerance — disproportionately communities of color. "Invest in what the community actually needs" operated below the surface of a mainstream consumer brand narrative.

**Why MVP matters:** MVP doesn't present as a diversity fund. They don't filter on founder identity. They filter on cultural authenticity and community resonance — which in practice means their portfolio skews toward brands with Black, urban, or multicultural roots. This is the "show, don't tell" version of the same problem uMODA addresses directly.

---

### i.am+ / i.am ANGEL Foundation — will.i.am (William Adams)
**Websites:** iamplus.com / iamangelfoundation.org

Will.i.am is the most explicitly AI-focused of all celebrity investor/advocates. He has positioned himself not as a passive LP but as an active builder around AI and STEM education for underrepresented youth.

**Notable investments and partnerships:** Beats Electronics (early investor, returned 10x when Apple acquired for $3B), Twitter (early), Ekso Bionics (exoskeleton mobility), Intel (brand partner and advisor), various AI creative tools.

**The i.am ANGEL Foundation** is based in Boyle Heights, Los Angeles, serving Latino and underrepresented youth with STEM programming. This foundation-to-pipeline model (philanthropy funding the talent that eventually feeds into tech) is structurally similar to the CLF community pipeline thesis.

**Why will.i.am is the most directly relevant cultural figure to uMODA's thesis:**

He is the most consistent celebrity advocate for AI specifically — and for the argument that Black and marginalized communities deserve equity stakes in the AI systems trained on their cultural output. From his public advocacy (2025–2026):

> Communities of color created the cultural content AI is being trained on — without compensation or ownership. They deserve equity stakes in the AI systems built on that foundation.

This is a direct parallel to the "cultural value created without ownership" thesis that runs through uMODA's situated fairness principle. The communities most affected by AI validity failures are the same communities whose cultural contributions trained the AI — without attribution or benefit.

**Relevance to uMODA:** Will.i.am represents a warm outreach pathway to the precise narrative uMODA occupies: not "help the disadvantaged" but "return ownership to the communities that created the value." His platform, his investment thesis, and his advocacy are all aligned with this frame.

## ATHLETE / ENTERTAINER INVESTMENT VEHICLES

---

### Thirty Five Ventures (35V) — Kevin Durant and Rich Kleiman
**Website:** 35v.com

Durant and Kleiman built one of the most diversified celebrity investment portfolios in tech. Rich Kleiman brings operational infrastructure most celebrity vehicles lack. Durant brings cultural validation and sourcing network.

**Portfolio (100+ companies):** Coinbase, Acorns, Robinhood, Postmates, Therabody, Whoop.

**Thesis logic:** Invest where Durant's network creates an unfair advantage. Less focused on community uplift, more on leveraging cultural capital for financial return. However, significant fintech exposure (Acorns, Robinhood) directly serves communities historically underserved by traditional banking.

---

### SpringHill Company — LeBron James and Maverick Carter
**Website:** springhillentertainment.com

The most sophisticated athlete business infrastructure in history. SpringHill Company (media, entertainment, content) valued at $725M; investors include Nike, Epic Games, RedBird Capital Partners, Fenway Sports Group. LeBron's "I Promise School" in Akron represents community infrastructure investment — building the human capital pipeline for children from his own community.

**Model:** Build owned media infrastructure that amplifies the cultural capital of Black athletes and creators, rather than deploying capital into others' companies. Creating the distribution layer.

---

### SC30 Inc. — Stephen Curry

Curry's thesis: financial access and wellness, particularly for women and communities excluded from wealth-building infrastructure.

**Notable:** Penny Finance (financial wellness platform for women, Curry co-founded) — fintech specifically targeting women systematically underserved by financial institutions. This is the situated fairness principle applied to fintech.

---

### Serena Ventures — Serena Williams
**Website:** serenaventures.com

Serena Williams' fund is the clearest example of a cultural GP model — a celebrity operating as an actual GP making investment decisions, not as an LP in someone else's fund. The portfolio is diverse in both founder identity and sector.

**Thesis:** Backs founders who are historically passed over — women, people of color, and those from non-traditional backgrounds — building companies with real defensibility.

## OWNERSHIP MODELS AND COMMUNITY BUILDERS

---

### Nipsey Hussle's Legacy: Vector90 and The Marathon Continuing Foundation

Nipsey Hussle (Ermias Asghedom) represents the most community-embedded investment model of any cultural figure. Vector90 was a co-working space and STEM incubator he built in the Crenshaw neighborhood of Los Angeles — directly in the community he came from.

**The stated thesis:** Own the block. Own the buildings. Own the business. Not as an investor in someone else's company but as owner of the physical and economic infrastructure of your own community.

After his murder in 2019, The Marathon Continuing Foundation has continued this work.

**Why Nipsey matters:** He represents a model of community ownership that is entirely different from any VC vehicle — neighborhood-scale, infrastructure-first, rooted in the principle that ownership must happen in the community, not for the community.

---

### Tyler Perry Studios — Tyler Perry

Tyler Perry built Tyler Perry Studios on 330 acres of a former Confederate Army base in Atlanta — a deliberate act of reclamation. Self-financed at $250M+ without outside capital, retaining 100% ownership. His studios are now an anchor of the Atlanta Black entrepreneurial ecosystem.

**Model:** The "build your own infrastructure" approach taken to its extreme. No LPs, no VCs, no co-investors.

---

### Rihanna — Fenty Beauty Ownership Model

Rihanna owns 50% of Fenty Beauty. At its peak valuation ($2.8B), this made her the wealthiest female musician in history. LVMH provided capital infrastructure; Rihanna retained half the equity.

**Fenty Beauty's founding thesis was also inclusive by design:** 40 shades of foundation at launch, explicitly addressing the cosmetics industry's historic failure to serve dark skin tones. The inclusion thesis was the product thesis.

**Model:** Negotiated equity rather than VC investment — retaining real ownership while using institutional capital infrastructure.

---

### Magic Johnson Enterprises — Magic Johnson

Magic Johnson was one of the first major athletes to build formal investment infrastructure for community-focused businesses — specifically designed to bring quality goods and services to underserved urban communities: Starbucks franchises in communities that had no Starbucks, movie theaters in neighborhoods without cinemas, SodexoMagic (food services to underserved institutional settings).

**Historical significance:** Magic proved that underserved Black urban markets were profitable, not charitable — a thesis the venture community has been slow to internalize.

## NON-TRADITIONAL INSTITUTIONAL INVESTORS WITH ALIGNED THESIS

---

### MacKenzie Scott

MacKenzie Scott has given away over $17 billion since 2020, making her arguably the single most impactful funder of Black and marginalized institutions in modern history. Her method is radical: no applications, no reporting requirements, no strings.

**Relevant recipients:** Howard University ($40M), Spelman College ($40M), Xavier University ($20M), NAACP Legal Defense Fund, Movement for Black Lives, Color of Change, UnidosUS, dozens of HBCUs and CDFIs.

**What makes Scott distinct:** She is dismantling philanthropic gatekeeping. Traditional foundations require extensive reporting, aligned strategy, and institutional conformity. Scott funds what is actually working in the community on the community's own terms.

---

### Robert F. Smith — Vista Equity Partners

Robert F. Smith is the founder of Vista Equity Partners, the largest Black-owned private equity firm in the United States ($100B+ AUM). Vista focuses on enterprise software — private equity buyouts and growth investments, not venture stage.

**Community investment signal:** Pledged to pay off the entire student loan debt of the Morehouse College Class of 2019 ($34M commitment). Founder of Fund II Foundation, which deploys capital to support Black male achievement.

---

### Mellody Hobson — Ariel Investments

Co-CEO and President of Ariel Investments, one of the largest Black-owned investment management firms in the US ($17B+ AUM). Ariel was founded by John Rogers Jr. in 1983 — one of the longest-standing examples of institutional-scale Black-owned investment infrastructure.

**Thesis:** Financial education and access to equity markets is the most durable path to generational wealth for Black Americans.

---

### Arlan Hamilton — Backstage Capital
**Website:** backstagecapital.com

Already covered in the main VC alignment research (Diverse Founders section). Hamilton's intellectual contribution beyond the fund: she has been one of the most vocal voices naming the structural problem — not that underrepresented founders lack ability or ideas, but that access to capital networks is restricted by proximity to existing wealth.

---

### Alexis Ohanian — 776
**Website:** 776.vc

Named after the year (per fictional lore) that Wakanda was founded in the Black Panther universe. 776 explicitly commits 10% of carry to social causes. Invests in diverse founders across technology, health, wellness, and creator economy. His marriage to Serena Williams and public advocacy for equity give him cultural credibility within communities traditional VCs cannot access.

## CORPORATE DIVERSITY VEHICLES

---

### SoftBank Opportunity Fund

SoftBank launched a $100M fund specifically for Black, Latinx, and Native American founders in 2020. Portfolio includes Clearco (revenue-based financing), Cityblock Health (healthcare for underserved urban communities).

**Structural critique:** A $100B+ fund created a $100M vehicle for underrepresented founders — 0.1% of AUM. The fund exists and is deploying capital; the structural implication is visible.

---

### Goldman Sachs One Million Black Women

$10B commitment ($5B direct investment + $5B philanthropic) for Black women entrepreneurs and economic development. Backs companies at various stages with focus on economic access and financial inclusion.

---

### JPMorgan Chase Advancing Black Pathways

$30B racial equity commitment including Community Development Finance to Black-owned businesses, HBCU investment, affordable housing, and small business financing in underserved markets.

## COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS (CDFIs)

CDFIs are the unglamorous backbone of community economic development — regulated financial institutions chartered to serve economically distressed communities, filling the gap left by traditional banks. Collectively they deploy billions annually into communities that VC never touches.

| Organization | Focus |
|-------------|-------|
| Hope Enterprise Corporation | Deep South, Black rural communities |
| Low Income Investment Fund (LIIF) | Affordable housing, early childhood infrastructure |
| Community Reinvestment Fund (CRF) | Small business lending in underserved markets |
| Opportunity Finance Network (OFN) | National CDFI network |
| National Development Council (NDC) | Community infrastructure financing |

## HBCU ENDOWMENT INVESTING NETWORKS

HBCUs represent the most concentrated and trusted institutional relationships with Black communities in America. When an HBCU deploys its endowment capital into a company, it sends a credibility signal no white-led VC can replicate.

- **Howard University** — Howard Venture Partners initiative
- **Spelman College** — LP in Zeal Capital Partners (covered in main alignment doc)
- **Morehouse** — Innovation and Entrepreneurship Center placing students in tech startups
- **Hampton University, Clark Atlanta, North Carolina A&T** — Building similar infrastructure

Atlanta is emerging as the most significant non-coastal Black entrepreneurship hub in the country: Tyler Perry Studios (anchor), OneTen (workforce development), The Gathering Spot (community/professional network), Decatur Angels (Black-led angel syndicate).

---

### Janngo Capital — Africa / Diaspora
**Founder:** Fatoumata Bâ, Managing Partner

Pan-African venture fund focused on gender equity and economic development across Africa and the diaspora. Invests in companies using technology to leapfrog infrastructure gaps — the same structural framing that applies to many underserved American communities. Check sizes: $500K–$5M.

**Significance:** The "diaspora capital" thesis — Black investors and organizations that see African and African-American communities as part of the same investment landscape — is an emerging framework that connects US-based situated fairness investing with global majority-world investment.

## MODEL TAXONOMY

These vehicles cluster into five models. Understanding the model determines the pitch frame.

| Model | Description | Examples |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| **1 — LP Access Vehicle** | Cultural leaders as LPs in someone else's fund. No independent deal selection; institutional-quality deal flow. | a16z CLF; various athlete LPs |
| **2 — Cultural GP** | Cultural figure as actual GP making investment decisions. Full autonomy; requires real fund infrastructure. | Serena Ventures, QueensBridge, Marcy Venture Partners |
| **3 — Ownership Platform** | Cultural figure builds and owns infrastructure rather than investing in others. Full equity retention; no dilution. | Factory Holdings, Tyler Perry Studios, SpringHill |
| **4 — Community Infrastructure Investor** | Invests in physical, educational, or financial infrastructure of the community. Slower returns; requires patient capital. | Vector90 (Nipsey), CDFIs, HBCU endowments |
| **5 — Corporate Diversity Vehicle** | Major institution creates a carve-out fund for underrepresented founders. Significant capital scale; often performative fraction of AUM. | SoftBank Opportunity Fund, Goldman One Million Black Women |

---

## WHAT CLF DOES UNIQUELY WELL

Andreessen Horowitz's Cultural Leadership Fund (CLF) combines Model 1 (LP access to institutional deal flow) with Model 4 (fees/carry routed back to community pipeline via nonprofit beneficiaries). It is the only vehicle in this landscape that has structurally encoded its own success into community benefit — the more the fund returns, the more capital goes to nonprofits advancing Black talent in tech.

**CLF LPs include (partial):** Jay-Z, Nas, Pharrell Williams, Kevin Durant, Diddy, will.i.am, and dozens of other cultural figures. Ben Horowitz built the cultural bridge — CLF is the institutional result.

## OUTREACH RELEVANCE TO uMODA

The entities most relevant for uMODA outreach from this landscape are those whose thesis includes:
- The "cultural value created without ownership" argument (will.i.am, Nipsey legacy)
- Community-first sourcing rather than tech-first (QueensBridge, Collab Capital crossover)
- Essential services for underserved populations (Magic Johnson Enterprises model, CDFIs)
- Proof that underserved markets are profitable, not charitable (QueensBridge, Backstage Capital)

**The strongest warm intro pathways** run through:
1. **will.i.am / i.am+** — direct thesis alignment on AI cultural value gap
2. **Arlan Hamilton / Backstage Capital** — already in the main alignment doc; crossover entity
3. **Collab Capital** — already in Tier 1; Jewel Burks Solomon's network includes many of these cultural LP networks
4. **HumanityAI ecosystem** (Kapor, Ford Foundation, Mozilla) — the bridge into HBCU and CDFI networks
5. **Reframe Venture / Project Liberty** — convening the responsible AI LP community that overlaps with several cultural fund LPs
